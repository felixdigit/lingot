An external artefact is read by someone who cannot ask you a question. That single fact sets every rule below.

1. **Depth is the default, not brevity.** The recipient cannot follow up, so the artefact stands alone -- exhaustive granularity, the full picture, the edges addressed. Under-scoping an outward-facing artefact is the failure this gate exists to prevent.

2. **Structure before prose.** Draft the skeleton -- the sections and what each carries -- and align on it before writing paragraphs. Then execute section by section, not one wall.

3. **Source every non-obvious claim.** External credibility is sourced; a figure or claim without provenance is a liability. Carry the source visibly -- a caption, a footer, an inline link.

4. **Match the studio voice + format.** Reuse the established template: the last artefact of its kind is the model -- its typography, its structure, its close. One studio, one voice; a new piece should look like it belongs to the set.

5. **Separate real from aspirational.** Mark what is built and true versus what is planned or claimed. Conflating them in an outward-facing document is the costliest error.

6. **Close on the honest verdict.** End on the real read -- the "so what," caveats included -- not a marketing flourish.

**Done** = it stands alone at exhaustive depth, every claim is sourced, it matches the studio voice, real is separated from aspirational, and it closes on an honest verdict.
